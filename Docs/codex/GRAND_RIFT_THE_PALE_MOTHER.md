## 1. What this is

**The Grand Rift's Mythic**, and the first named threat in [[death-canyon]].

A spider the size of a barn, assembled out of bone. Not her own. Every plate, every leg segment,
every socketed joint came off somebody, and she did not grow them — she **fitted** them, and she
is still fitting them, and that is why she is still getting bigger.

She carries a **cage**: a ribbed hollow slung between her legs, closed, dark, and full.

**She has never once defended herself.** Read every behaviour she has again with that in mind and
it all changes meaning. She does not attack — she *interposes*. She puts her body between you and
the cage. She smothers the fire because fire is what kills them. She backs into the deep gas
because you cannot see in it and they can. She is not a monster fighting for its life.

She is made out of other people's dead, and she is a good mother, and there are four hundred and
eleven of them in there.

## 2. Owner rulings

**RULING 1 — She does not make the gas, and she never gets to.**
`death-canyon`'s open question — *"What produces the green light and purple gas?"* — stays open.
Hunters swear she breathes it. Bone Market boat crews say she is where it comes from. She is not,
and after she dies the canyon is exactly as poisonous as it was the week before, at the same
depths, on the same schedule. She is the region's loudest wrong answer, the same shape
[[elias-vey]] holds in Bloomfall, deliberately, because it is a good shape.

**Never write the gas as hers. Never write her death as thinning it.**

**RULING 2 — The light is blue-green, because Mackenzie said blue-green.**
The fissure luminescence is a cold marsh-teal, not a sick yellow-green — corpse-light, the colour
of a lit swimming pool at night, under rolling violet gas. This is her palette, in her word, and
it does the job canon needed anyway: it holds Death Canyon apart from Bloomfall's sick filtration
green and Magic-Torn's purple at a glance. `death-canyon`'s existing "unnatural green
luminescence" is satisfied and sharpened, not contradicted.

**RULING 3 — "Undead" is a real word here and she is what it means.**
The Riverlands' Ossuary Rites are a dignified covenant between generations: the dead stay in the
family's keeping, bone is contract, nothing supernatural about any of it. The one horror that
trade has a word for is **bone that will not settle** — bone that goes on working after the rite.
That word is *undead*, Death Canyon is where it was coined, and she is the reason anybody needed
it. No reanimated corpse. No necromancer. **Never write a caster behind her.**

**RULING 4 — The Bone Market Families are her supplier and her customer, and never her author.**
See §9. Nobody built her. Do not let anybody take credit for her.

**RULING 5 — Three roads, and the Brood is the choice.** Burn them, or let them go. There is no
bargain and no cure. Regional law adds the outcome that is not a player choice: **nobody came**.

## 3. What she is

**Nine metres across the legs.** Wide and low — a working platform on legs, never a towering
vertical monster. No soft parts anywhere: no hide, no eye, no organ, no wet surface. A standing
assembly of pale plates fitted so precisely the joints work, and the fit is the frightening part,
because a fit that good was made by something with an opinion about it.

**She has no face.** Nothing to read an intention off. That lock is absolute — the moment she has
a face she becomes an ordinary monster.

Blue-green fissure light enters her underside and comes out through the gaps between plates in
thin uneven seams, so at two hundred metres through violet gas she reads as a pale lantern.

**Something is moving in the cage the whole time.** It is on screen for the entire first phase.
Most players will not look. That is the design working.

---

## 4. The fight, and why it is the fight

**She does not have a health bar. She has a lid.**

The bar on screen is the cage. Every point of damage you do to her body is a plate off the thing
holding four hundred and eleven children shut, and the game never says so once.

### The three target groups

| Target | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **The eight legs** | Break one and she loses reach and speed. Break all eight and she goes down. | Slow. Lots of them. She can re-seat a broken leg if you let her stand over a plate pile. |
| **The Carry** — the plate armature over the cage | Real damage. The obvious body shot. | Every plate off the Carry is a latch off the cage. |
| **The Seams** — where the blue-green light escapes | **The best damage in the fight by a wide margin, and they glow.** | They are the cage. They are literally the gaps in it. |

A player who has read nothing will shoot the glowing weak point, because thirty years of games
have taught them to, and they will have the cage open in ninety seconds. **The tutorial for this
fight is every other game they have ever played, and it is wrong.**

### Which means the kill has three different endings

| How you killed her | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Seams first** (fast, obvious, glowing) | The cage comes apart before she stops moving. The Brood pours out **while she is still standing**, and she keeps fighting. Both at once, in the gas, with your fire mostly spent. This is the disaster run, and most first attempts will be it. |
| **Body damage, no discipline** | She dies, the legs fold, and then the cage opens. A clean phase break. This is the intended hard fight. |
| **Legs only, cage untouched** | She goes down with the lid still on — and you can hear them in it. Burn the cage closed. **Nothing gets away.** This is mastery, it is brutally difficult, and it is the only outcome where the count reaches zero. |

Standard gamer shapes throughout — break the parts, don't hit the weak point, kill the adds. The
only thing that is new is what they mean.

### The vents, and the economy that makes it thrilling

The gas is heavy and inert and does not burn. What burns are the **fissure vents** — fourteen of
them across the arena floor. Ignite one and it throws a column of flame up through the gas.

**The vents are the fight's real resource.** They are the area damage a party that brought none
can still use, they are what kills the Brood, and there are exactly fourteen of them.

**Settle** is why. She lowers the cage onto a burning vent and puts it out, and heals off it, in
one unhurried animation — and that is not a boss healing itself, that is a mother standing on the
fire. Every vent she smothers is one you do not have in phase two.

So the fight has a genuine strategic tension with no new systems in it:

- **Kill her fast** (Seams) → most vents left, and the cage opens early into a fight you have not
  finished.
- **Kill her slow** (legs) → the cage stays shut, and she smothers vents the whole time, and you
  reach phase two with four vents and four hundred children.

There is no correct answer. There is only the one your party can actually execute.

## 5. Phase two — the Pale Brood

Several hundred bone spiders, each about the size of a hand, and they are the colour of the gas.

Not camouflaged and not invisible — the same hue, the same value, the same softness at distance.
In violet gas they are violet. Over a fissure they are teal. **Your only tell is that the gas is
moving wrong.**

### The rules, and they are flat

| Rule | Why it is absolute |
|---|---|
| **Bullets do nothing. Blades do nothing.** Total immunity, never a 90% reduction. | A resistance teaches a player to shoot harder. An immunity teaches them to change tools. The encounter is a damage-type gate, and a gate with a gap is not a gate. |
| **Area damage kills them.** Magic, fire, explosives, thrown gas, a lit vent. | Standard, legible, and the canyon itself provides it. |
| **One tick, one death.** No broodling has meaningful health. | The fight is **coverage**, not damage. You are not out-damaging a swarm, you are covering ground. |
| **They pick one person — the nearest — and they climb.** Boots, shin, chest. | A swarm that spreads evenly is a debuff. A swarm that picks somebody is a horror. |
| **Damage is a stack called Taken, and it is slow.** Ten stacks and you go down. | Nobody is one-shot. Everybody is being eaten. |

### The count

**She has four hundred and eleven.**

Phase two does not show a health bar. It shows **a number going down.** Every vent, every spell,
every grenade takes some. The fight ends when it reaches zero, or when the rest of them get past
you into the deep gas.

Whatever number is left when you walk out of Death Canyon **is recorded**, and it is the same
number the [[bone-market-families]] have been failing to balance for four generations.

### The sound, which is the whole horror

Death Canyon hums. The fissures do it, constantly, everywhere, and after ten minutes down there
you stop hearing it the way you stop hearing a refrigerator.

**The Brood does not make a sound. It blocks one.**

When they cross a fissure the hum goes out in that spot, and the shape of the silence moving
across the canyon floor is the only warning you get. **You fight this phase by listening to a
noise stop.** It is learnable, it is fair, it is locatable in the mix, and the first time a player
works out what the quiet means is the moment this creature lands.

### Four beats, because she asked for extra extra scary

1. **The gas goes quiet.** Above.
2. **They are patient.** Reach clear stone and they do not rush you. They stop at the edge of the
   gas and wait, and they can wait longer than you can stand there.
3. **The cage was full the entire first phase**, in plain sight, and the player chose not to look.
4. **The passenger.** One broodling attaches to a random party member on the way *in* — before
   the fight, in the approach, silently. No damage. No icon. No sound. It rides. It wakes up in
   phase two, already inside your formation, already past everything. Players who inspect each
   other on the walk in will find it. Nobody inspects anybody on the walk in.

**Reassembly** is the fail-forward: leave them alive long enough and they start carrying plates
back. They will rebuild her in front of you, out of the fight you just won — and if a player goes
down and is not picked up, they build her out of that too.

## 6. Extremely difficult, and genuinely winnable

The difficulty contract, and every line of it is a promise to the player:

- **No unavoidable damage anywhere in the encounter.** Every attack is telegraphed or audible.
- **Nothing one-shots.** Every death is the end of at least four seconds of ignored information.
- **There is always an out.** A party with zero area damage can still win using the vents. The
  canyon is the weapon and working that out is the intended clever solve.
- **The fight is long.** Eight to twelve minutes, and phase two is three of them without a pause.
- **The wipe is not a reset, and this is the mercy.** Her plates grow back — she rebuilds her
  armour out of whatever is on the canyon floor. **Her children do not.** However many you burned
  stay burned. Come back and the count is lower, and she is more careful, and she will not leave
  the deep gas as readily as she did.

That last rule is what makes an extremely hard fight winnable without a difficulty slider: it is
attrition across attempts, it costs the player nothing but time, and **it is diegetic.** She gets
harder to reach and easier to survive at the same time, for the same reason, and the reason is
that she is running out.

A party that beats her on the fourth attempt has not been given a handout. They have worn a
mother down.

## 7. The arena

Three readable gas bands, and the encounter runs across all of them:

| Band | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear shelf** | Broken shelves above the pool line | You can see. She is slow here and the Brood will not follow. No cover, and no vents. |
| **Working depth** | Violet gas at chest height, teal light from below | The real arena. All fourteen vents. She uses Fissure Step. The Brood is unreadable. |
| **The pool** | Gas over your head in the deepest fractures | The existing environmental hazard at full strength. She heals here. Do not follow her in. |

## 8. Kits

**Phase one — the Walk.** Sweeping Foreleg · Shed Plate · The Drag · Fissure Step · **Settle** ·
Cradle Slam.

- **The Drag** — one foreleg out of an opaque gas wall, from nothing, at reach. This teaches the
  player that the gas can contain her, so that phase two can teach them it contains worse.
- **Fissure Step** — she walks into the deep gas and you lose her completely. Not invisibility;
  the gas is opaque and she is patient. The tell she is coming back is the gas.
- **Settle** — the vent smother and the heal, one animation. §4.

**Phase two — the Brood.** Opening the Cage · Gaswalk · Climb · The Quiet · Reassembly.

## 9. Where the bone comes from

This is what makes her a Martino creature rather than a spooky skeleton, and it is already in the
codex — nobody has to invent it.

[[riftgate]] is the relic leg: tannin-dark water running northwest out of [[heartland]] toward
[[grand-rift]] country, held by the [[bone-market-families]], carrying **rift-relics, bone-goods,
grave salvage and the quiet coffins of the dead trade** down to buyers who do not ask where the
Grand Rift ends and the graveyard begins. It passes [[charnel-lock]], where the toll is always
exactly correct. It passes [[bonefire-picket]], where the beacon burns rendered bone-oil. It is
witnessed at [[redletter]], on paper that does not expire when you do.

The trade has run both directions out of Rift country for generations. **The tally has never
balanced**, and the Families have carefully never asked why, because a discrepancy you have not
asked about is not yet a liability, and the Families are excellent at law.

**RULING 4, plainly.** They did not build her. Nobody built her. What they did was buy and sell
across a border with something on the other side of it that pays in bone-goods and takes delivery
in bone — politely, for a very long time, with the paperwork in order.

**Writing rule.** Never name a culprit, never write a Family member who knew, never let a document
confess. The Ossuary Rites are honest and stay honest: the desecration is what happens to covenant
bone four hundred kilometres upriver of lawful keeping, and the distance is the whole cruelty. If
anybody says it out loud, the design collapses into a conspiracy.

## 10. Taken, and who it actually costs

A broodling that stays on you takes bone. **Taken** stacks, ten and you go down, and surviving
with stacks leaves a real lasting injury.

Which a party can afford to fix. The Forge rebuilds you whole; that is what the Forge is for.

**So the Brood is not a threat to you. It is a threat to everybody who cannot buy a body.** The
Picket's keepers do not come back from what you walk off. That is the class marker this world
runs on, landing on a monster fight, and it is why the bounty is posted where it is posted.

## 11. The bounty, and the three roads

Posted at [[bonefire-picket]] — the last fixed light on the leg, kept by people who render bone
for a living and have opinions about what is up there.

The posting says, in the plainest words the codex is capable of: **bring fire.** Players will not
read it. A party that kills the Pale Mother with rifles and blades and then dies to her children
has experienced the encounter correctly, and the design does not owe them a save.

| Road | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Cage never opened** | Legs only, then fire on a closed cage. The count reaches zero. Death Canyon has no Mother, and the Families' tally balances for the first time in living memory, and somebody at Charnel Lock notices and says nothing. |
| **Killed her, burned most of them** | The count is a number and the number is not zero. It is written down. Something is still down there and you know exactly how much of it. |
| **Killed her, left** | Reassembly finishes without you. There is a Pale Mother in Death Canyon by the season, built out of you-do-not-know-who, and she is bigger. **And one of them left with you.** |
| **Nobody came** | Not a player choice. She goes on fitting. |

## 12. Harvest and consequence

| Piece | What it is | What taking it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A Settled Plate** | The one bone on her that stopped working. Proof that settling is still possible. | Nothing. The clean drop. |
| **Brood-glass** | What a broodling leaves when fire takes it: a small clear bead of fused bone. One per kill, so **your pouch is the count.** | Selling it at Charnel Lock tells the Families exactly how many she had. They will price it. They will not stop buying. |
| **Cage Rib** | One rib off the brood chamber. Crafting stock. | You are carrying a piece of the thing that held them, and the Ossuary Rites have a view about that. |

| Not the `mythical` parent | — | Locked to the Lizzarnix by its own dossier and by test. Nothing here touches it. |

**Not seeded, does not spawn.** She is in the deep gas, and no player action creates her.

## 14. What this does not answer

- **What makes the light and the gas.** Open. She is the strongest wrong answer anybody has.
- **Who has been selling into Death Canyon, and for how long.** Never named, never confessed.
- **Whether the covenant bone on her was sold or stolen.** Both are on her. Nobody can tell which
  is which any more, and that is the point of the Rites.
- **Where the passenger gets off.** Not answered here. Planted, and owed.
- **Campaign act, unlock, mandatory status.** Not selected.

## Appendix A — the original design

*Preserved as spoken. This is the source; everything above is its reconciliation.*

**Contributor: Mackenzie Martino.** Relayed by the owner, 2026-09-04.

> "Daddy I want a spider in the blue green place and i want it to have lots of little ones when
> it dies"

Every word of that is load-bearing above. **A spider** — arachnid first, faceless, never a
skeleton standing up. **The blue green place** — RULING 2; the fissure light is her colour and it
is the one that separates this canyon from two other regions. **Lots of little ones** — four
hundred and eleven, counted, and the count is the phase-two HUD. **When it dies** — §4, where the
health bar turns out to have been the lid the whole time.

Her father added the rest of the shape on the same day: made of bone; the little ones swarm onto
the player; they cannot be killed with bullets or melee, so you need magic or explosives; they
blend into the gas; and it should be extra extra scary. All of it is above, unsoftened.
