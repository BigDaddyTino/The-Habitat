# The Eight Trees — what the simulations found

## The tuning pass (owner's rulings, applied and measured)

The owner ruled on all five decisions the first campaign raised. Every
ruling became a real change — two of them new talent nodes in the live
trees — and the campaign re-ran to measure it. Before → after:

1. **The Cypherist's body is findable.** Uplink's text now carries the cost
   — *"the feed traces both ways: whoever looks hard enough finds the body,
   and the body is soft"* — and the model agrees: hard concealment became
   modest concealment, and the found body takes hits like a body with no
   frame around it. **91% → 77% of duels.** The Archon's Sky Cavalry (86%)
   is now the roster's top duellist, which is a defensible crown: flying
   cavalry should frighten people.
2. **The Bastion got teeth.** A new Shieldwall node — **Answer in Kind**
   ("every hit your plates ate this fight is a debt; pay it back, with
   interest") — plus honest weight on Knuckle Plate, Pain Ledger and the
   class's own rifle. **21% → 36% of duels, 30% → 48% solo.** Still the
   hardest thing in the game to put down; no longer unable to win the
   fights it cannot lose.
3. **The Procurator can hold a road alone.** A new Command node — **Your
   Own Orders** ("the wheel turns for a column, or for one person: you") —
   and a sidearm used deliberately. The Fire Plan holds ~22% of duels and
   real solo ground; the Supply Line remains the game's most
   party-dependent build **by its own choice of branches**, which its
   dossier should say out loud.
4. **Corruption pays like a bargain, not a checklist.** The free power at
   phases five and six came down, and the institutional costs are now
   mechanical: tells make you easier to read and hit from phase four,
   nobody sells plates to a phase-five, and at Turning nobody billets with
   you — between-fight care halves. The all-in Red Line stack fell **99% →
   75%**, and the phase curve grew a shape: a dip at Tremor, a genuine
   **valley at phase five** where Drift's price bites before Turning pays,
   then the phase-six payoff. Climbing is a decision now.
5. **Parties stopped steamrolling.** Party content is elite — veterans of
   the same fourteen kinds, greater in number AND quality — and the
   signature party fight is the siege: waves with no recovery between.
   **100% everywhere → 66–93%**, and composition finally matters: the
   all-damage party cracks at the siege (40%) while the disciplined ranged
   party holds it (92%).

Everything below is the first campaign's full analysis, kept as the
baseline the tuning was measured against.

---

Run 2026-08-31 against the approved trees. The raw campaign log is in
`MARTINO_SIM_RESULTS.md`, regenerated by
`scripts/sim-talent-balance.ts --write`; this document is the design half:
what the numbers mean, and what they ask somebody to decide.

Every build in the campaign is assembled from the real talent data, so a
build that broke a prerequisite, took both sides of a fork, or overspent the
144-point budget stopped the run instead of producing a number. Sixteen
archetypes, eight species-and-origin combinations, seven trades, the seven
phases of the ladder, eight encounters drawn from canon's fourteen kinds of
opposition, and a day of four fights with only a resupply between them.

## What the design claimed, and whether it held

**"The ladder pays."** Confirmed, and it is the campaign's cleanest result.
A clean build wins 47% of its encounters; the same build at phase six wins
58%. Every phase from three upward is worth more than the one below it. The
corrupted branch is not a consolation prize — it is a progression path, which
is exactly what the design said and the first thing the numbers agreed with.

**"Weak alone, unbeatable in numbers."** Half confirmed. Parties win 100% of
doubled hard encounters regardless of composition; the Procurator's fire plan
is real. But alone the Procurator wins 6% of duels and survives 0% of days —
which is not weak, it is absent. See the decision below.

**"Nobody owns everything."** Confirmed structurally: the archetypes spend
58–77 of 144 points on nodes and still leave ability ranks unbought. No build
in the campaign came close to clearing its tree.

**"Species is identity, not power."** Confirmed, and pleasingly so. Across
single fights the spread between the best and worst species is one percentage
point. Across a day of four fights it opens to twelve — Latent 50%, Human
45%, Returnee 38% — because mend pace and the Conductivity ceiling only
matter under attrition. Species decides how your week goes, not your fight.

**"An infused caster is a person with a rifle when the crate is empty."**
Confirmed for guns, not yet for doses. Both Maverick builds spend 77–79% of a
day's rounds on their worst option — they run out of ammunition and keep
fighting with one iron. No caster ever ran out of pool or charges, because
fights end before an economy can bite.

## Five decisions the numbers put in front of you

**1. Uplink is the strongest thing in the game.** The Cypherist's Remote War
build wins 91% of duels, survives 100% of days, and wins the fourth fight of
the day 100% of the time. It stacks concealment, damage mitigation and bodies
that are not its own, and nothing in the campaign reaches the operator. The
world already has the counters — a Spector's Blind Spot, a Conduit naming the
cast, a Dampening Coil — but none of them costs Uplink anything mechanically.
*Recommendation: the operator's body is somewhere. Make it findable and
killable, or make the drones degrade when the operator loses the feed.*

**2. The Bastion is a wall with no teeth.** 21% of duels, 30% of solo
encounters, 0% survival across a day. Its mitigation works — it is the
hardest archetype to put down — but its damage is a rifle, so fights it
cannot lose it also cannot win, and attrition eventually takes it.
*Recommendation: one repeatable damage node in Shieldwall, or accept
formally that the Bastion is a party piece and say so in its dossier.*

**3. The Procurator cannot participate alone.** 6% of duels, 0% of days, and
its best solo showing is 11% on hard ground. The class fantasy is right and
the party numbers prove it, but a player who takes this class and walks into
the world by themselves has no game to play.
*Recommendation: one self-sufficiency node in Command — or an explicit
codex line that this class is not a solo class, which is a legitimate
choice as long as the enlistment desk says it out loud.*

**4. The corrupted stacks are as strong as the audit feared.** Red Line on
the Red Ladder at phase six beats a clean Bastion 99% of the time. Never Here
plus Already a Ghost wins 96%. Vessel plus Edge wins 83%. Nothing in the
combat model restrains a phase-six character, because everything that
restrains them in the fiction is institutional: doors close, the ACA comes,
no Forge will bind you, and every teacher but the Choir and the Covenant
stops taking your calls. *Recommendation: those costs have to be mechanically
real in play. If corruption only ever shows up as power in a fight, the
ladder stops being a bargain and becomes a checklist.*

**5. Parties need harder content than the encounter set has.** Four
level-100 characters beat doubled hard encounters 100% of the time in every
composition tested, including the one without a medic and the one without a
commander. *Recommendation: party content wants tripled encounters or
encounter types the model does not yet have — sieges with a reserve clock,
fights with civilian obligations, and the horn.*

## What the simulations could not see

- **Suspicion, standing, licences and the law.** The most expensive things a
  corrupted or unlicensed character carries are institutional, and none of
  them exist in a combat model. This is the single biggest reason the
  phase-six numbers should be read as "power in a fight", not "power".
- **A whole day is four fights here.** Canon's real attrition unit is a
  campaign, and reclamation, the Essence economy and the Forge's reserve are
  the things that actually grind a party down.
- **Roughly half the nodes.** About 250 of the ~450 talent nodes carry
  arithmetic in the model; the rest are narrative — real in play, nothing to
  measure. Every "How it plays" line from the gameplay audit still stands.
- **The enemy statlines are invented** to canon's descriptions. They are
  internally consistent and nothing more.

## The content this produced

Balance findings are content, which was the point of running them. Four
things to write:

1. **The counter-operator scene.** If Uplink needs a body to find, somebody
   in the world hunts operators for a living — a Bureau contractor, or an
   Iron Saints team that specialises in it. That is a faction wing and a
   quest chain nobody had written.
2. **The Maverick's ammunition problem** is a story: a gunslinger is a
   logistics client, and the Glassload node should make rounds rather than
   only choose their payload. That is a Chemistry-adjacent relationship, and
   the Maverick's dependence on a Procurator or a hand-loader is a party
   dynamic worth writing.
3. **Institutional teeth for the ladder.** The phase-four checkpoint, the
   phase-five licence review, the phase-six billet refusal — each is a scene
   the world already implies and the numbers now require.
4. **The Returnee's twelve points.** A people who mend at half pace lose a
   day differently to everyone else. Their medics, their standing orders and
   their attitude to a long campaign are all downstream of one number, and
   it is the kind of detail the codex likes.
