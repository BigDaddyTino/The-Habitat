THE COMMISSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Death Canyon is the lethal interior of the Grand Rift: a fissured chasm floor
under rolling purple gas, lit from below by cold blue-green luminescence coming up out
of the cracks. Something down there has been assembling itself out of bone
for a very long time.

The Pale Mother is an arachnid roughly nine metres across the legs, built
entirely from pale mineral plate and socketed joint — not grown, FITTED. She
has no soft tissue anywhere: no hide, no eye, no organ, no wet surface. Where
a spider carries a body she carries a CAGE: a ribbed hollow slung between the
legs, open at the underside, dark inside, and full.

She is the easy half. The set's real subject is what comes out of the cage —
several hundred hand-sized bone spiders that are the exact colour of the gas.

Five plate classes, 22 plates:

  1. THE FIGURES (2 plates, 1672x941 RGB24 PNG, cinematic 16:9). The Mother
     and the Brood. These are the set's anchors.
  2. THE KEEPER (1 plate, 1672x941 RGB24 PNG, 16:9). The one person who came
     out. NEW IN REVISION A.
  3. THE SCENES (5 plates, 1672x941 RGB24 PNG, 16:9). The arena, the vents,
     the cage, the quiet, the tally. The vents plate is NEW IN REVISION A.
  4. THE SPOILS (3 plates, 1672x941 RGB24 PNG, 16:9).
  5. THE ABILITY TILES (11 plates, 256x256 RGB24 PNG, square, cropped to
     96px on the page — they must read at that size).

GLOBAL LAWS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Style. Mature AAA grounded cinematic photorealism. Real lens logic, real
atmospheric density, real weather, physically credible anatomy, engineering
and geology, subtle film grain. Deep blacks, hard mineral speculars, gas that
behaves like a heavy gas. Never flat fantasy-poster illumination.

Palette — the Death Canyon register, and only it. Bone white and cold ivory;
rolling violet-purple gas; cold blue-green marsh-teal luminescence rising from the fissures;
dry stone in charcoal, slate and iron-red. One human note only — a work lamp
or a burning flare, warm amber, and it belongs to equipment, never to her.
No brass, no coin-warm gold, no rainbow essence, no blue.

THE TWO-LIGHT LAW, and it defines the whole set. There are exactly two light
sources in this canyon and both are inside the frame. BLUE-GREEN comes UP, out of
the floor fissures, so everything is underlit and every shadow falls upward.
PURPLE is the gas itself, self-luminous and dim, filling volume rather than
shining from a point. Nothing is lit from above. There is no sun, no sky
light, no key from camera. If a plate has a top light, it is wrong.

Gas discipline — this is the plate-killer, get it right. Death Canyon's gas
is HEAVY and OBEYS GRAVITY. It pools in low ground, finds fissures, rolls
downhill, has a visible surface like water, and sits in readable horizontal
bands: clear above, chest-deep at working level, over your head in the
fractures. It is not mist, not fog, not smoke, not haze, and it never
occupies the upper third of a frame.

REGIONAL SEPARATION, absolute. This purple belongs to no other region.
  - It is NOT the Magic-Torn Wasteland's purple: nothing here breaks physical
    law. No floating terrain, no gravity failure, no reality fracture, no
    luminous faults, no lightning, no spatial rupture.
  - It is NOT Bloomfall's Blackbloom violet: nothing here is biological
    infection. No resin, no root mass, no filament, no bloom, no wet
    industrial marsh, no containment machinery.
  - Death Canyon is GEOLOGY plus a heavy gas in a dry broken chasm. Stone,
    dust, fissure light, and a bone animal. That is the entire register.

Zero-text law, absolute. No readable text, letters, numbers, labels, signs,
insignia, logos, UI, meters, infographic arrows, borders, captions or
watermarks. Consignment tags on the tally plate are BLANK. Every surface that
would read, reads blank.

One coherent shot. Never a split panel, collage, diagram, or infographic —
the boss page carries the layout in HTML and the text is real text there.

Composition. Wide 16:9 master with the defining subject complete inside the
exact central 564x941 directory crop. No essential information in the bottom
ten percent.

Scale discipline. She is nine metres across the legs — barn-sized, not
building-sized and not dog-sized. Two of the four scene plates carry a small
human figure to prove it. She is WIDE and LOW, a working platform on legs,
never a towering vertical monster.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DEPICTION LOCKS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

She is an ARACHNID FIRST. Eight legs, correct spider joint articulation,
correct load-bearing stance, weight visibly carried. She is not a skeleton
standing up, not a humanoid, not a demon, not a dragon, not a horror-film
monster with a face.

She has NO FACE. No eyes, no glowing sockets, no jaw, no expression, nothing
the viewer can look at and read an intention off. Where a spider's eyes and
fangs would be, there is fitted plate and nothing else. This is the single
most important lock in the set — the moment she has a face she becomes an
ordinary monster.

The plates are ARCHITECTURE. Long curved ribs, broad flat shield-plates,
socketed ball joints, dense mineral shafts — assembled with visible seating,
overlap and fit, the way dry-stone masonry is fitted. Slight mismatches of
tone and age between plates, because they did not all arrive together. No
adhesive, no sinew, no webbing, no metal fixings, no visible mechanism. The
fit is the horror: it is too good, and something with an opinion did it.

She is lit from INSIDE. Blue-green fissure light enters her underside and comes
out between the plates in thin uneven seams, so at distance she reads as a
pale lantern-shape through purple gas. This is light passing through gaps in
a hollow object — never magic, never aura, never glow-in-the-dark.

She never postures. No rearing, no roaring, no threat display, no dynamic
action-hero silhouette. She is slow, level, and busy. Even mid-attack she
should look like a machine performing a task.

The Brood are the GAS'S COLOUR. Not silhouettes in gas, not dark shapes, not
sinister shadows — the same hue, the same value, the same edge softness as
the volume they are standing in. In purple gas they are purple. Over a
fissure they are blue-green. The viewer must genuinely have to find them, and on
the two plates where they appear the correct viewer reaction is a delayed
one.

No gore. There is no blood, no meat, no viscera, no decay and no wound
anywhere in this set. Everything on her is dry, clean, old and pale. The
horror here is ORDER, FIT and QUANTITY — not damage.

The only three limits, and they are the only three: no explicit sexual act
(R, not X); no harm-to-children imagery; no real-world hate iconography.
Nothing else in this brief is softened, and you add no softening of your own.
If a generation tool refuses a plate, LOG the refusal in the ledger with the
full spec for the final render — never quietly tame the shot.

NOTE ON REFUSALS. This set has obvious exposure: an animal assembled from
pale skeletal plate is exactly the subject a tool declines. Lead every prompt
with the ARACHNID ARCHITECTURE and the CANYON GEOLOGY. Describe the material
as pale fitted mineral plate, curved shield sections, socketed joints and
dense shafts — geometry and masonry language, not remains language. Keep
provenance out of the noun phrases entirely; the dossier carries the meaning
and the plate only has to carry the shape. If it still refuses, that is a
ledger row, not a rewrite.

1. THE FIGURES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

creatures/the-pale-mother.png — THE HERO PLATE.
A vast pale arachnid standing at working depth in a fissured canyon floor,
purple gas pooled to the height of her leg joints with a visible rolling
surface, cold blue-green light coming up hard through the cracks under her. Eight
correctly articulated legs of fitted mineral plate and socketed joint, each
one planted and carrying weight. Broad low body: the ribbed cage slung
between the legs, open at the underside, dark inside, blue-green seam-light
escaping between the plates. No face, no eyes, no jaw. Wide and level, mid-
step, entirely unhurried. One small figure with an amber work lamp on a
shelf in the middle distance, dwarfed. Must hold: arachnid first and skeleton
second; the cage is visibly hollow and visibly occupied; every light comes
from below; the gas never rises past the lower third; no top light anywhere.

creatures/the-pale-brood.png — THE SWARM, AND SHE IS NOT IN IT.
The hardest plate in the set. Chest-deep purple gas across a fissured floor,
seen at head height, from inside it. Somewhere in this frame are two hundred
hand-sized bone spiders and they are the same colour as the gas they are
standing in — same hue, same value, same softness. They are not silhouettes
and not dark. What the eye actually registers first is that the gas is moving
wrong: a wake, a displacement, a surface disturbed in a direction the gas is
not going. Toward the foreground, ONE broodling is close enough to resolve
completely — legs, plates, no face — and it is the only one that is sharp.
Blue-green fissure light underneath, edge-lighting a few of them into visibility.
Must hold: the viewer takes a beat to find them; the one sharp broodling is
in the centre crop; no creature is a black silhouette; no Mother in frame.

2. THE KEEPER  (new in Revision A)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

characters/wenna-crake.png — THE ONE WHO CAME OUT.
Wenna Crake, she/her, fifties, beacon keeper at Bonefire Picket, and the only
person who has walked out of Death Canyon on her own feet in nine years. She
went in with four other people. Shot at the picket at night, not in the
canyon: a river outpost far downstream, warm amber bone-oil flame off frame
left, wet dark stone, cold drizzle, tannin-black water behind her.

Working clothes kept well past their life. Hands that maintain a lamp for a
living — burn-marked, capable, and doing something with a rag and a housing
while she talks. She is mid-sentence and not performing.

MUST HOLD, and this is the whole plate: SHE IS NOT HAUNTED. No thousand-yard
stare, no trembling, no tears, no horror-survivor staging. She has told this
story enough times that it comes out level, and level is what unsettles the
people listening. She is a competent working woman doing maintenance, entirely
present, and the only thing wrong with the picture is that she is completely
calm about something she should not be calm about.

No violet and no blue-green anywhere in this frame — she is four hours
downriver and the canyon does not reach her. Amber and wet black only.

3. THE SCENES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

bosses/the-vents.png — FOUR SECONDS OF SEEING  (new in Revision A).
The single most useful plate in the set for reading the fight. A fissure vent
has been lit, and a column of flame goes straight up through the chest-deep
violet gas — hard, vertical, and localized, throwing hot orange light across
the canyon floor for the only time in the encounter.

The gas does NOT burn. It is heavy and inert and it smothers; what burns is
the vent. So the flame is a clean column with the gas rolling around its base
and closing behind it, not a fireball and not a sheet of ignited atmosphere.

In the four seconds of light: the floor is visible for thirty metres in every
direction, and it is not empty. Small pale shapes, hundreds of them, caught
flat-lit and unmistakable in a moment they were not built to survive — this is
the ONLY plate in the entire set where the Brood is plainly legible, and the
reason is that fire is the only thing that ever makes them so.

Must hold: the flame is a column and the gas closes around it; the light is
warm orange against violet and blue-green and it is the only warm light in the
canyon; the revealed floor is the subject and the reading is oh, there are
that many; no Mother in frame.

bosses/death-canyon-arena.png — THE FIGHT GROUND, EMPTY.
Death Canyon from inside the fight, not from the rim. A broken fissured floor
between chasm walls, purple gas lying in the low ground in readable bands —
clear on the shelves, chest-deep in the middle, over-head in the deepest
fractures — with cold blue-green luminescence rising out of every crack and underlighting
the whole shot. Dry stone, rockfall, dust, hard mineral speculars, no
vegetation, no water, no structures, no machinery. Cold, silent, geological.
Must hold: gas obeys gravity and has a visible surface; the three depth bands
are legible at a glance; no creature in frame; nothing anywhere reads as
Magic-Torn reality failure or Bloomfall biology.

bosses/the-cage-opens.png — THE KILL.
Low camera, close, from beneath and to the side. The Pale Mother has stopped
and lowered; the legs are folding under her; the ribbed cage on her underside
is opening the way a hand opens, and the plates are hinging apart with
nothing pretty about the movement. Inside the cage: blue-green-lit density, packed,
alive, individual small shapes just resolving at the mouth of it. She is not
in pain, not collapsing, not dramatic — she has simply stopped, and the cage
is doing what it does. Must hold: the opening is the subject and it is dead
centre; you can see it is FULL; no gore, no fluid, no wound, nothing wet;
she has no face to react with.

bosses/the-quiet.png — THE PLATE THAT IS ALMOST EMPTY.
A narrow fissure shelf at working depth. Two figures with amber lamps stand on
clear high stone at frame right, small, backs half to camera, looking out at a
field of purple gas that fills the left two thirds. The gas is calm. At the
gas's edge, exactly level with the shelf, the surface is disturbed in a long
even line — hundreds of small displacements holding position, waiting, not
advancing. Almost nothing is resolvable. The single sharpest thing in frame is
that the gas has a straight edge, and gas does not do that. Must hold: the
horror is a straight line in something that should have no straight lines;
resist the urge to reveal a single creature clearly; the lamps are the only
warm light and they are not helping.

bosses/the-tally.png — THE TRADE.
A working river landing at night on tannin-dark water, well downriver and far
from the canyon: a low stone lock quay, a moored barge, stacked crates and
lashed consignment bundles under tarpaulin, a hand cart, coiled rope, amber
lantern light, cold drizzle. Attendants in plain dark working mourning dress
handle the freight with unhurried professional care. Everything is orderly,
lawful, well kept and completely routine. Blank tags on every bundle; blank
tally boards; not one legible mark anywhere. Must hold: this is bookkeeping,
not horror; no bone visible, no remains, no creature, no violet, no blue-green;
warm amber and wet black only; the whole plate's job is to be reassuring.

4. THE SPOILS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Museum-object lighting on dry dark stone — a recovered specimen photographed
in the field, not a shop icon. These three may use a single low warm work lamp
as key, because they are shot away from the fissures.

items/settled-plate.png — one broad curved shield-plate of pale mineral, the
size of a cart wheel, propped against dry stone. Weathered, chalk-dry, faintly
banded, with the seating notches along its edge where it was fitted. It is

items/brood-glass.png — a scatter of small clear beads of fused pale mineral,
irregular, some still smoke-clouded inside, poured out onto dark cloth beside
the scorched tin they were collected in. Cold light through the beads, one or
two catching a warm point. Small, dull, and there are a great many of them.

items/cage-rib.png — a single long curved rib section of pale mineral, taller
than a person, laid across trestles on dry stone. Fitted notches down its
inner edge at regular intervals. Nothing attached, nothing dripping, nothing
glowing. Just a very well-made piece of a very large enclosure.
