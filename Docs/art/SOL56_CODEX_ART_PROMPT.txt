SOL 5.6 — CODEX KEY ART RUN: THE CHARACTER BIBLE'S 41 EMPTY SLOTS
==================================================================

You are producing key art for the Martino Story Codex, not for a cinematic.
There is no timeline, no dialogue, no sequencing — 41 single still images,
each one the hero image of one codex dossier. Your brief for each image is
already written: it is the entry itself. Read the dossier before you draw it.

THE MANIFEST IS THE JOB
-----------------------
Docs/MARTINO_CHARACTER_BIBLE_ART.md lists all 41 slots: entry title, slug,
the EXACT output file path, and a one-line description of the subject.
That list is the complete scope. Produce every slot, save each file at
exactly the path the manifest gives — filename, directory and extension are
load-bearing (the app finds art by convention: the file named for the slug
IS the wiring; there is no registry to update). Species art goes in the
races/ directory — the folder name on disk is historical, do not "fix" it.

FORMAT
------
- Landscape, 16:9, minimum 1600 px wide. House convention is 1672x941 —
  match it. PNG.
- One image per slot. No variants, no contact sheets, no collages.

ART DIRECTION — TWO AUTHORITIES, IN ORDER
-----------------------------------------
1. The codex entry "the-look-of-the-world" (the law):
   - Near-future fusion worn casually. Enchanted firearms beside security
     cameras, crystal ammunition from ordinary crates, a field-infusion rig
     maintained like a truck engine. Nobody in-frame is amazed by magic-tech.
   - Magic leaves its own scars: glass bowing before it breaks, gravity
     forgetting itself, strange scarring — never ordinary explosive damage.
   - Old power, new kit: the fusion test applies to every subject.
   - Mature, not performative. People who might not survive the next five
     minutes.
2. Docs/BLOOMFALL_REACH_VISUAL_BIBLE.md (the shared material language):
   AAA cinematic realism; wet charcoal concrete, oxidized steel, blackened
   ceramic, dull brass fittings; Essence as contained cyan-to-storm-blue
   light that is always physically motivated (conduit, vessel, vapor,
   mineral charge — never omnipresent neon); overcast daylight, cold work
   lights, amber emergency lamps — light always has a source.

HARD PROHIBITIONS (every frame)
-------------------------------
- NO text in frame. No signage that reads, no tickers, no labels, no fake
  alphabets, no baked footers or frame numbers. Abstract hazard bands and
  blank plates only.
- NO UI. No health bars, menus, icons, map overlays.
- NO fantasy register. No wizard towers, robes, staffs, steampunk ornament,
  cyberpunk neon signage, glossy sci-fi plastic. Engineering, not smithing.
- No real-world brands or broadcaster identities.
- No omniscient hero shots for horror subjects — this world is witnessed at
  human scale.

THE FOUR SHELVES, AND WHAT EACH DEMANDS
---------------------------------------
SYSTEMS (14) — Each is a scene that teaches the system without a caption.
  The manifest's one-liner names the scene. Two rules:
  - the-six-pillars and its six children (thermodynamics, kinetics,
    structure, biologics, cognition, resonance) are ONE SET: design the
    visual grammar once — same framing logic, same registry/institutional
    feel — and let the six differ only in subject. They are shelves in one
    licensing registry, not six unrelated paintings. Do the-six-pillars
    first and derive the children from it.
  - Match the existing systems shelf (apps/web/private/codex-art/systems/
    has 30+ finished examples). Study three of them before you start.

SPECIES (5) — returnees, carriers, chartered, the-unregistered, the-latent.
  They join a shelf with illustrated members (human, humanoid, beasts,
  mythical in the same directory). Match THAT set — portrait-of-a-people
  compositions — NOT the Bloomfall creature plates. These are all human
  peoples; the differences are history, bearing, and context, not anatomy.
  Do not invent visible mutations, ears, or glowing marks — especially for
  the-latent, whose entire nature is that nothing shows.

ITEMS (6) — The history is the subject, not the object. The hole in the
  torso plate a medic cut a shard out of; the spent shell case in a
  rating's hand; the page torn out of a ledger; a grave marker with one
  word's worth of space (shown so no word is legible). An object catalog
  shot is a failure here.

CHARACTERS (16) — Lowest priority, do them LAST. All sixteen are PROPOSED
  with placeholder names; art commits a face nobody has written a scene
  for. Rules:
  - The four Kestrel command staff (medic, mechanic, quartermaster, scout)
    are the only ones worth polishing — they are canon-adjacent to the
    prologue. Do those four first within this shelf.
  - Sixteen DIFFERENT faces, ages, and builds. Reuse of one face across
    extras has been a defect in your work before — it will be checked.
  - Correct faction kit per dossier (Kestrel field gear, Stormglass range
    kit, Sovereign Guard drill dress, Ascendancy clinic whites, Tropic
    Pearl rider harness). Wrong-kit extras have also been a defect before.
  - The captured rider's hypogriff (if shown) uses the codex sheet
    silhouette — eagle forward anatomy, horse hindquarters. No invented
    creature anatomy.
  - Faces read as people under stress, not portrait-studio heroes.

ORDER OF WORK
-------------
1. the-six-pillars, then its six children (the set grammar).
2. The remaining 7 systems.
3. The 5 species.
4. The 6 items.
5. The 4 Kestrel command staff, then the 12 teachers.

SELF-QA BEFORE YOU REPORT DONE (view every image at full resolution)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- Zero legible text anywhere in any frame, including debris and signage.
- Every light source visible or implied; Essence glow always contained.
- The six pillar images sit side by side as one set.
- No two characters share a face.
- Every file at its exact manifest path, landscape, >=1600 px wide.
- Nothing that would read as another franchise's fantasy.

Then re-run the audit so the manifest updates itself:
  pnpm --filter @habitat/web exec tsx scripts/audit-character-bible.ts --write-art-manifest
A clean run reports "41 of 41 have key art; 0 slots are empty" and the
manifest lists nothing. Deliver: the audit output plus a one-line-per-image
ledger of what each frame depicts. Do not write to the codex, the database,
or any file other than the 41 image paths and your ledger.
