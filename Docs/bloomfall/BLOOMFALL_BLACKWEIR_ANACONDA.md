## 1. What this is

**The first MYTHIC in the game.** A named, two-phase, region-defining bounty at [[blackweir]] —
the Living Marsh's filtration weir — whose subject is a man who is still alive inside it and
still working.

Twenty years ago **Dr. Elias Vey** was a Southreach biologist studying Essence-adaptive
organisms beneath what is now the Living Marsh. When the storage banks entered uncontrolled
resonance and the facility locked down, he had two options and took the second: evacuate, or
activate the prototype. The prototype was a filtration organism designed to absorb enormous
volumes of contaminated Essence and convert it into ordinary biological work. It functioned. It
did not have enough biological mass to stabilise.

So he fed it the mass it needed.

He was conscious for all of it. That is the part everyone skips.

## 2. Owner rulings

Four decisions were taken on this design. Two of them changed it.

**RULING 1 — He claims the marsh, and he is wrong.**
Vey states, in his own voice, that he is the Living Marsh: that its coordination is his
coordination, that Heartfen's channels close because he closes them. He means it entirely. He is
wrong. Nothing in twenty years of Meridian telemetry has ever agreed with him, and after he dies
the channels keep closing on schedule, at Heartfen, exactly as before.

This preserves the region's locked law without touching a word of it —
*"there is no confirmed hive mind, telepathy, central organism, or marsh god"* — while giving
that law its most dangerous witness: a thing that lived inside the system for two decades and
came back with an answer the instruments refuse to support.

**Never write the marsh as his.** Never write Ansel as wrong. Never write his death as breaking
the marsh's coordination. What his death breaks is Blackweir's *engineering* — the weir, the
channel discipline, the deliberate sacrifices — which was genuinely his and is genuinely
irreplaceable. The distinction is the whole design.

**RULING 2 — Two roads: kill, or leave him in the water.** No bargain path, no
take-his-place path. You put him down, or you walk away knowing exactly what is still down
there. Regional law adds a third outcome that is not a player choice: every Bloomfall crisis
must be able to resolve with **nobody came**.

**RULING 3 — Elias and Tomas Vey are brothers.** [[tomas-vey]], the surviving Southreach
shift-control engineer, is his brother, and has not said his name aloud in twenty years.

**RULING 4 — MYTHIC designation, and he is the origin of Adaptive Mutation.** See §6 and §8.

## 3. What he actually did

Elias Vey's specialty was **Essence-adaptive organisms**: living systems that could absorb and
redirect energized Essence without immediately mutating past usefulness. His institutional
position was that the Reserve's problem was never Essence. It was that Southreach was trying to
*control* something alive with interlocks, schedules, and load orders — machinery's answers to a
biology question.

He was proven right in the worst available way.

When [[the-bloomfall]] began, he was in Sublevel 4 with a prototype that had never been run at
scale. The emergency lockdown gave him the length of one corridor to decide. He activated it,
and when the organism began to fail for want of substrate, he opened his own tolerance and let
it take him as the substrate.

The changes were not fast. His skeleton lengthened over weeks. His skin plated. His nervous
system did not fuse with the filtration network so much as *volunteer* into it, node by node,
each junction a decision he was awake for. By the time an emergency team reached the laboratory
there was no person in the room. There was a serpent wrapped around the reactor chamber, and it
did not attack them, and it did not answer.

**He is not the cause of the Bloomfall.** He acted after the cascade had already begun, with a
device intended to contain it. Canon's guardrail on his brother — *"Vey is not secretly
responsible for the Bloomfall"* — now reads as a family sentence rather than a single man's, and
neither Vey is the culprit. `DEEP_MYSTERY_TRUTH` remains `DEFERRED`.

## 4. What he is now

The prototype worked. That is the horror of it. Bloomfall Reach's southern front stopped at the
ancient wetlands, and part of the reason it stopped is a man who decided to become a filter.

Over twenty years his body absorbed Blackbloom from soil and water while the organisms around
him adapted to process what he could not. He grew. Sections of Blackweir's filtration
network — root mass, channel gates, sink beds — are now continuous with his nervous system. When
Blackweir closes a channel ahead of a surge, that is him. When Blackweir sacrifices a section of
its own root field to save the beds downstream, that is him choosing which part of himself to
lose.

**The amputated arm was his arm.** [[the-purge-window]] already records Blackweir amputating a
section and relocating its front without reference to the people upstream. That was written as
ecology and read as ecology. It is also a man deciding, alone, at speed, which limb to spend.
Nothing in that arc needs rewriting; it simply becomes true a second way.

He is roughly **forty to sixty metres** in his true form and still growing, because nothing has
told him to stop.

## 5. Why he attacks

He does not experience hostility. He experiences **contamination**.

Aegis crews cutting resin out of the beds. Meridian teams removing specimens. NDD personnel
trying to bring old Southreach systems back up. Hunters harvesting the mutated fauna he spent
two decades tuning. To him these are not people with claims; they are foreign material
introduced into a functioning system, and the system's response to foreign material is
sequestration.

So the hunters stop coming back.

Their bodies are not eaten. They are recovered — supine, arms at their sides, feet toward the
water, in the shallows above the resin beds where the light reaches. Each one carries a tag
pressed into a resin disc at the throat, bearing a sample number. **The numbers are sequential.
They are past three hundred. There are no gaps in the sequence.**

He has been running a study for twenty years. He kept the records. He kept them well.

## 6. The two claims

Everything about this fight turns on the fact that he makes an enormous claim that is false, and
never bothers to make the small one that is true.

**The claim he makes: "I am the marsh."** He says it plainly, without grandeur, the way a man
states his job. It is false. Heartfen's coordination predates him by an unknown span, operates
outside every stretch of network he touches, and continues unchanged after his death. Dr. Keira
Ansel's position survives him completely: *coordination is measured fact, consciousness remains
unproven.* He is not the answer to the Living Marsh. He is the loudest thing that has ever
claimed to be.

**The claim he never makes: he is why the Reach adapts.** [[adaptive-mutation]] — the ladder
every creature in Bloomfall Reach climbs when you wound it and fail to kill it, the ladder the
entire regional economy of risk is built on — is his prototype, loose in the biosphere for two
decades. Meridian can match the five mutation families back to the organism's tissue signature.
He has never mentioned it, because to him it is not interesting. It is the part of the design
that *worked*.

So: he is wrong about the thing that would make him a god, and right about the thing that makes
him the author of the region. Nobody can dismiss him and nobody can believe him. That is the
correct amount of trouble for a Mythic to cause.

**Writing rule.** The origin of Adaptive Mutation lands as *evidence*, never as his testimony —
an assay result, a tissue match, a Meridian conclusion arrived at over his objection or his
silence. If he ever says it out loud, the design collapses into a boast.

## 7. The brothers

[[tomas-vey]] is his brother.

Tomas was on shift-control duty when the banks went. He survived an evacuation route that others
did not. Canon already gives him his exact function — he *"can authenticate terminology, not
motive"* — and that has always been about his employer's language. It is now also about
handwriting.

He can identify his brother's hand on a prototype requisition. He has been able to for twenty
years. He has told nobody, and he does not volunteer it now: he has spent two decades being the
man whose testimony is incomplete, and he understands precisely how a sentence like *my brother
is down there* would be filed.

He does not say the name. He will confirm it if you put the requisition in front of him and ask
a question he cannot answer any other way. He never asks you to spare him and he never asks you
to finish it.

**Do not write Tomas as a quest-giver, an ally in the fight, or a grieving witness at the water.
He stays at [[cairnwood-camp]]. He is where the bounty is posted, and that is the whole cruelty.**

## 9. The arena, and the seven states

The fight is Blackweir itself: shallow filtration channels, broken rail bridges, sink beds, and
the standing pylons that carry contaminated Essence through the weir. Water depth changes with
the reactor state and with what he does to the gates. Blackbloom vents fire on their own
schedule and force movement.

**The reactor state is the fight's weather**, and it is canon's, not new. `reactor-cycles`
already runs seven states. The design's own seven map cleanly onto them:

| Design state | Canonical `reactor-cycles` state | At Blackweir |
|---|---|---|
| Stable | **Dormant Interval** | Low hazard. Water at its lowest. The approach is walkable and he is hardest to find. |
| Charging | **Stabilization Cycle** | Pylons take load. He grows more responsive; ability tempo rises. |
| Charging (upper) | **Sector Restart** | Old gates cycle. Routes open and close under you mid-fight. |
| Residual | **Venting** | Plume cover. Concealment for both sides; his sensing degrades and so does yours. |
| Purge | **Purge** | The sequence he is built for. He anchors and draws hard; contaminated Essence floods the channels. |
| Overloaded | **Overflow** | Saturation past the weir's design. Every wound he takes closes faster. |
| Critical | **Containment Breach** | Authored crisis only, never a roll. The barrier fails and the fight stops mattering. |

**The Blackbloom saturation band is a separate axis** and stays canon's four —
Residual / Active / Surge / Bloomstorm — never a fifth persistent band.

## 9a. How the encounter starts

**He is always there. He does not spawn.** He is under Blackweir at every hour of every day, and
no player action creates him — that is what "authored, not seeded" means, and it is the whole
difference between him and the five Aberrants the ladder can seed at 1%.

What the player controls is **whether he comes up**, and the trigger is the thing he is about:
you surface him by being the contamination.

| Interference | What it is |
|---|---|
| Working the resin beds | Harvesting [[blackweir-resin]] — the licensed activity the whole zone exists for |
| Cutting filtration roots | Taking [[sinkroot-fiber]] out of the barrier |
| Lifting a specimen | Removing anything he has catalogued |
| Dropping a pylon | Sabotaging the supply that is also the containment |

Enough of that in one visit and the water opens. **This is why every name in the Wardens' column
is a harvester** — nobody on that list went looking for him, and that is the point of the list.

`reactor-cycles` sets the terms. A Dormant Interval keeps him deep and hard to draw; a Purge
brings him up already anchored and drawing. See §9 for the full state table.

**He does not reset, and there is no successor.** Break contact and he goes under and heals a
rung, and whatever you left him at is what meets you next time. That is canon's standing rule for
named threats — *"named threats retreat when wounded rather than despawning, do not casually
respawn, and a successor is a separate authored outcome rather than the same creature returning
unexplained"* — and there is no authored successor here. Kill him and Blackweir has no keeper,
permanently.

The bounty at Cairnwood does not summon him. It tells you where to go and pays if you finish it.

## 10. Phase 1 — Dr. Vey, the Mire Stalker

Heavily serpentine, still recognisably built on a person. He wears what is left of a Southreach
research coat and equipment harness, twenty years into the water, fused at the shoulders where
he stopped being able to take it off. He moves through shallows and ruin by ambush and reach.

**He speaks.** Not taunts — corrections.

> "Stop disturbing the filtration network."

> "You are introducing foreign material."

And when you have hurt him enough that the shape stops being worth maintaining:

> "Then I will remove you myself."

### He learns, using the region's own law

He does not get a bespoke learning system. He runs **[[adaptive-mutation]] inside a single
fight** — which is correct, because it is his.

- He carries the **Prisma** rule from the ladder's Advanced rung: all five damage types halved
  except one hidden weakness, which takes **+25%**.
- Every time you let him **disengage into deep water and return**, the hidden weakness moves. The
  ladder's central law — *hurt it, fail to finish it, and it comes back knowing* — is compressed
  from a season into ninety seconds.
- Behavioural reads on top of that: repeated dodges in one direction get answered in that
  direction; repeated healing after a specific attack gets that attack replaced with an
  interrupt; retreating into water is met with the water.

His attacks are precise because there is a scientist running them, and he is not angry.

**Kit:** Lunge · Tail Whip · Acid Spit · Ambush Dive · Shedding Strike · Grab and Drag.

## 11. The transformation

At roughly half, the player is winning. Then he stops moving.

His arms retract into the torso. The legs fuse. The spine runs out. The coat tears off him and
goes into the water. The Blackweir pylons come up to full load and push contaminated Essence into
him in a volume nothing living should survive, and his scales split to let the body underneath
finish opening.

Then the camera pulls back far enough to show that the humanoid was never his form. It was the
shape he was **holding**, for twenty years, because at some point it stopped being possible to
tell the difference between maintaining a human silhouette and remaining a person, and he was
not willing to find out which one he had lost.

He lets go of it to kill you. That is what it costs.

## 12. Phase 2 — The Blackweir Coil

Forty to sixty metres of anaconda that is also infrastructure. Old Southreach identification
plates set into the scales. Essence conduits running the length of the spine. Laboratory
equipment grown into the body at the angles it was standing at. Blackweir resin between the
plates. Eyes that light when something moves. Sections of him visibly continuous with the marsh,
because they are.

He is not living in the Living Marsh any more. He is part of it — the engineered part, the part
that was built.

**Kit:** Devouring Surge · Coil Crush · Toxic Flood · Tail Tsunami · Bile Eruption · Venomous
Roar · Submerged Stalk.

**Mechanics.** Arena-wide abilities with less telegraph and more consequence. The pylons can be
sabotaged to weaken him, at the price of the containment they are doing. Blackbloom buildup on
the party accelerates. Harvested ground has fewer safe zones, because you took them. Escaping
and returning heals him into a higher threat rung — his own ladder, used against you.

## 13. The bounty

Posted at **[[cairnwood-camp]]**, which is where his brother lives.

One posting, three issuers, three incompatible sets of instructions, and nobody willing to
withdraw:

- **[[aegis-extraction-consortium]]** wants the resin quota protected and the interference
  stopped. It does not care what state the thing is in.
- **[[meridian-arcane-institute]]** wants it studied, intact, and approached by nobody. Its own
  standing file on the subject is a single line — **DO NOT ATTEMPT TO CONTACT VEY** — which is
  either a safety instruction or an admission, and Meridian will not say which.
- **[[wardens-monster-hunter-guild]]** wants it dead, because three hundred tags in sequence is
  not a research programme, it is a body count.

Standing moves with whichever of them you satisfy, and the three cannot be satisfied together.

### Contradictory records

Southreach's personnel files disagree about Elias Vey, and the disagreement is real rather than
sinister:

> *Dr. Vey died during the cascade.*

> *Dr. Vey evacuated successfully.*

> *No personnel were present in Sublevel 4.*

This sits beside [[three-failure-reports]] and [[the-last-safe-reading]] as one more strand of
the same incompleteness — clocks, loads, and interlocks that cannot share one timeline. It
proves the official account incomplete. It identifies nobody, and it must never be written as a
clue to the cause of the Bloomfall.

## 15. Separation from the region's other named threats

Each of these is a real risk of the new content flattening old content. Each has one line of
canon holding it apart.

- **[[old-drowner]]** owns *hydrology* at [[drowned-intake]]: it moves water. The Anaconda owns
  the *filtration net* at Blackweir: it moves contamination. Canon line: Old Drowner is the only
  thing in the marsh Vey has never been able to reach, and he has stopped trying.
- **[[the-bellwether]]** is an ecological-intervention question and explicitly **not a boss**.
  Killing it collapses a signal network nobody built. Killing Vey ends a system somebody
  designed, on purpose, at a cost he named in advance. Ecology versus engineering.
- **[[the-last-shift]]** is human-origin with personhood unresolved and no voice. Vey is
  human-origin with personhood *asserted* — by him, loudly, in sentences. The pair are the two
  ends of the same question and must never be written as the same answer.
- **[[switchmother]]** was built by someone else. He built himself.

# **Dr. Elias Vey — The Blackweir Anaconda**

Before the disaster, **Dr. Elias Vey** was one of Southreach Energy Reserve's lead biologists. His specialty was **Essence-adaptive organisms** — living systems capable of absorbing and redirecting energized Essence without immediately suffering mutation.

He believed the Reserve's biggest problem wasn't Essence itself.

It was that humanity was trying to **control something alive**.

### The Resonance Cascade

Twenty years ago, when the Southreach Reserve began destabilizing, Vey was stationed beneath the future Living Marsh studying an experimental filtration organism.

When the resonance cascade occurred, the facility went into emergency lockdown.

Vey had one choice:

**Evacuate, or activate the prototype.**

He activated it.

The experiment was designed to create an organism capable of absorbing massive amounts of contaminated Essence and converting it into harmless biological energy.

It worked.

But there wasn't enough biological material for the system to stabilize.

So Vey used himself.

His body began changing.

His skeleton elongated.

His skin hardened into scales.

His nervous system fused with the filtration network.

His Essence signature became indistinguishable from the surrounding wetlands.

By the time the emergency teams reached his laboratory, there was nothing human left inside.

Only an enormous serpent coiled around the reactor chamber.

---

# **The Living Marsh**

Here's the interesting part:

**Vey didn't die.**

He became the first successful biological containment system.

The marsh around Blackweir began changing because of him.

His body absorbed Blackbloom from the soil and water, while the organisms around him adapted to process the remaining contamination.

Over twenty years, his body grew.

And grew.

Eventually, sections of the Living Marsh's filtration network became connected to his nervous system.

That's why the marsh seems to behave with **coordination**.

It isn't just an ecosystem.

**Something is coordinating it.**

---

# **Why He Attacks the Player**

Vey isn't simply hostile.

He sees outsiders as **contaminants**.

Aegis scavengers removing Blackweir resin.

Meridian researchers extracting specimens.

NDD personnel attempting to reactivate old Southreach systems.

Hunters harvesting mutated creatures.

Every time people interfere with the marsh, his aggression increases.

Eventually, bounty hunters begin disappearing.

Their bodies aren't eaten.

They're found carefully arranged near the Blackweir filtration structures.

Almost like **specimens.**

# **Phase 1 — Dr. Vey, The Mire Stalker**

This is where I'd really lean into your humanoid serpent design.

He hasn't completely abandoned his human form.

He's roughly humanoid, but his body has become heavily serpentine.

## The really cool lore twist

The contradictory Southreach records could actually be intentional.

Some records say:

> **"Dr. Vey died during the cascade."**

Others say:

> **"Dr. Vey evacuated successfully."**

Another says:

> **"No personnel were present in Sublevel 4."**

And one classified Meridian document simply says:

> **"DO NOT ATTEMPT TO CONTACT VEY."**

The player eventually discovers why.

**Dr. Vey has been alive for twenty years.**

He's been underneath the Living Marsh the entire time, maintaining the containment system.

So when players finally kill him, they aren't just defeating a bounty boss.

They're potentially killing **the thing that has been keeping Bloomfall Reach's contamination from reaching the ocean.**

That could make the Blackweir Anaconda one of the most important bosses in the entire Bloomfall Reach region.
